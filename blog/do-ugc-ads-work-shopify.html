Do UGC Ads Actually Work for Shopify Stores?

Adam Nagy 14 July 2026 7 min read

UGC ads don't automatically work better than polished ads for a Shopify store. They work when the hook is strong enough to earn a hook rate above roughly 25% to 30%, and they underperform polished ads when the product needs a demonstration or trust signal that a phone-shot testimonial can't deliver. The "4x to 6x higher conversion" stat you'll see everywhere comes from companies that sell UGC production services, not from independent data.

The format isn't what makes an ad convert. The hook is. A UGC-style ad with a weak first three seconds gets scrolled past exactly like a polished ad with a weak hook does. What actually separates a working UGC ad from wasted production budget is the same thing that separates any working video ad: whether it earns a hook rate above 25%, a hold rate above 40%, and a message people actually believe.

Below is when UGC genuinely outperforms polished creative, when it doesn't, and the metric that tells you which one you're looking at before you've spent a cent on media.

When UGC ads genuinely outperform polished creative

  • 01

    The product benefits from a visible demonstration

    A before/after, an unboxing, or a "here's how it actually works" moment reads as more credible coming from a real person than from a studio set. If your product has a visual proof point, UGC is often the better format to show it.

  • 02

    The price point is low enough that a casual recommendation feels credible

    A $30 product recommended casually by someone on camera feels believable. A $400 product recommended the same way can feel underwhelming, the format undersells a considered purchase.

  • 03

    Your audience treats polished ads as a red flag

    Younger, more ad-literate audiences on Meta and TikTok often scroll past anything that looks like a traditional commercial. UGC's rough edges are a feature here, not a limitation.

  • 04

    You need creative volume for testing

    UGC is cheaper and faster to produce at scale than studio shoots, which means you can test more hooks and angles for the same budget. More tests, more chances to find a genuine winner.

When polished creative still wins

UGC isn't the safe default. A few situations where a professional shoot still outperforms it:

  • 01

    The product needs credibility a phone camera can't provide

    Skincare with clinical claims, financial products, or anything where trust in the brand matters more than trust in a stranger often needs the polish that signals a legitimate, established business.

  • 02

    Your brand's whole positioning is premium

    If your product sells on craftsmanship or luxury, a rough UGC ad can undercut the exact perception you're paying to build everywhere else.

  • 03

    The hook is weak regardless of format

    UGC doesn't rescue a boring opening. If the first three seconds don't earn attention, the ad fails whether it was shot on an iPhone or with a full crew.

How do you tell if a UGC ad is actually working?

Look at hook rate and hold rate before you look at ROAS. A high hook rate doesn't guarantee sales, but a low one guarantees the ad never gets the chance to sell anything.

Hook rate What it means
Below 20% The opening isn't working. Rebuild it regardless of format
20-25% Workable, but there's room to sharpen the first three seconds
25-35% Strong performance for either UGC or polished creative
35%+ Excellent. Whatever this ad is doing, scale it

A high hook rate doesn't guarantee a good outcome on its own. It just means people are watching. Whether that attention turns into a healthy customer acquisition cost still depends on your offer, your product page, and the rest of the funnel behind the ad.

Frequently asked questions

What are UGC ads?

UGC ads are paid ads styled to look like organic, unpolished content, usually a person talking directly to camera about a product, rather than a traditional studio-produced commercial. The format is meant to read as a peer recommendation rather than an advertisement.

Do UGC ads actually convert better than polished ads?

Not automatically. UGC ads convert better when the product benefits from a visible demonstration or the audience trusts a casual recommendation more than a polished one. They convert worse when the hook is weak, since the format doesn't fix a boring first three seconds, or when the product needs the credibility a professional shoot provides.

What is a good hook rate for a UGC ad?

The same benchmark applies to UGC and polished video ads: above 25% is considered solid, above 35% is strong, and below 20% means the opening needs work regardless of how the ad was produced. Hook rate is the percentage of people who keep watching past the first three seconds.

How much do UGC ads cost?

Typically far less than a professional shoot, often a few hundred dollars per video versus several thousand for studio production, since UGC ads use creators filming on their phones rather than a full production crew. Cost varies by creator experience and how many revisions are included.

Is UGC a thing in Australia?

Yes. Australian Shopify brands increasingly source UGC content from local creators or existing customers, and the format performs the same way here as anywhere else. What matters is hook rate and message fit, not where the creator is based.
